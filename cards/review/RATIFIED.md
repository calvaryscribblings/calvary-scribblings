**Captive:** a kidnapping, an arrest, a prison or a locked room that holds someone against their will, and the story turns on it, not a passing word. Feeling "trapped" in a job, a marriage or a life is a metaphor and does not count. **Grave:** a death, a funeral or a burial the story turns on. The other six motifs are as R1 defined them. ## The short answer

**The library honestly fills 53 of 54**: 3 picture cards and 50 story cards. **One slot is left empty, and it is Captive.**

- **Captive: 5 of 6.** 2 stories turn on it as their main motif, and 3 carry it honestly as a fair alternate:
  - *Free Will* (primary, strong): The narrator, dressed as a beggar, is seized by men rounding up the mentally ill and locked in their van; the whole story builds to that capture and ends on it.
  - *The Number Thirteen* (primary, strong): Armed officers seize Dayo at the market, drive him to an unknown building and hold him for hours as a wrongly identified drug supplier until he pays to bail himself; the whole story turns on this arrest, which he then reads as the curse of thirteen.
  - *The Man I Met At Nineteen* (alternate, fair): the narrator is arrested and tells the story from prison; the arrest is the payoff of the secret, so Secret stays its main motif.
  - *Anomaly* (alternate, fair): Chike holds Nonso tied up on the kitchen floor for the reckoning; the story still turns on the abused child, so Child stays its main motif.
  - *The Call* (alternate, fair): the whole story runs on a ransom demand for the daughter Edet abandoned. **Flag:** the kidnapping turns out to be a hoax staged by her cousins, which is why it is only fair. Child stays its main motif.
  - **All 5 are Short Stories**, and 3 of the 5 are by Dera Okaro. That is what the library holds; it is not a choice.
  - **Considered and declined** (no one is held, or the story does not turn on it): *Witness Me*: the arrest of HorseShoe and Yam Leg is the closing beat; the story turns on witnessing the murder; *Maid of Honour*: Kodi is handcuffed only after the shooting, and the story does not turn on the arrest; *Proof of Life*: the police escort Jemima to their car only in the closing lines; *1967*: the villagers are herded to the square to be shot that afternoon: a round-up for an execution, not a holding; *Hold Your Breath and Count to Ten*: the arrest and the binding vines are passing moments; it turns on the judgement at the bridge; *Brenda*: the abduction is staged, and the narrator is Brenda herself; *How Are You, My Friend?*: Halimat disappears and is found dead; she is never shown held. Every "trapped", "cage" and "prison" used as a figure of speech was declined too.
  - **The series:** Beta Princess (Part One, strong: Sibry and her servants are chained and marched to Silverclaw) and The Doherty Confessions (Instalment I, fair: the police come to arrest Victoria, and she jumps before they can) both carry Captive as an alternate. Their picture cards keep the motif each series turns on most (Secret), as ruled in R1.1.
